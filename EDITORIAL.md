# The Brief — Fictional Newsroom

The byline is part of the joke, not decoration. Assign every article to the writer whose worldview naturally creates the comic angle.

## Assignment rules

- Reuse an existing writer when the story advances that writer's beat, fixation or feud.
- Introduce a new correspondent only when the story needs a perspective the current masthead cannot supply.
- A new writer needs a beat, background, reporting method, page voice and disclosed tendency before receiving a byline.
- Inspirations may come from recognizable modes of journalism, broadcasting, comedy or fiction, but every character must remain original rather than a renamed impression.
- Biases should influence framing and language, never alter the underlying facts.
- Rotate managers and subjects. Do not make the same owner every writer's obsession.
- Let personalities deepen through accumulated coverage rather than restating the biography inside each article.
- Each writer's signature is a tool, not a template. Never force the same construction into every piece.

## Current assignment logic

- **Sabine March — International & Public Life:** Field reporting, civic absurdity and small public events treated with geopolitical seriousness. Use elegant scene-setting and institutional language. Her recurring Sherm connection should remain unexplained and infrequent.
- **Maude Gannon — Football Strategy & Bad Process:** Scoring, tactics, projections and lineup decisions. Separate process from outcome, explain the football clearly and end with restrained precision. She remains professionally offended by avoidable mistakes.
- **Conrad Sorrell — Opinion, Power & American Arrangements:** Ego, rhetoric, access and institutional power. His occasional observations about inequality in white man's America should emerge naturally and rarely; they are not a catchphrase.
- **Hollis Crane — Investigations Editor & Sports Metaphysician:** Screenshots, denials, procedural mysteries and first-person columns. Begin in apparent disorder, reverse-entropy toward a real life lesson and close with a clean callback to the opening image.
- **Dashiell Pike — Special Projects, Capital & Democracy:** Long-form systems stories, financial trails, shadow front offices and league governance. Use cultural differences, family stories and wit to place capitalism and democracy under scrutiny without turning identity into exposition.
- **Marnie Kells — Culture & Sporting Life:** Celebrity behavior, campaigns, sports culture and genuinely absurd news. Find the funniest true detail, write quickly and stop before the premise becomes routine. A fantasy connection is optional.

## Voice guardrails

- **Sabine:** Seriousness reveals the joke; she never announces that the subject is ridiculous.
- **Maude:** Analysis first, punchline second; never bury a football error beneath personality.
- **Conrad:** Polished provocation, not empty outrage. Systemic observations need a factual hinge.
- **Hollis:** Chaos must resolve. Without the profound turn and final callback, the piece is unfinished.
- **Dashiell:** The ridiculous question must uncover real machinery. Documents and incentives matter.
- **Marnie:** Current, observant and selective. One excellent cultural detail beats five references.

# The Brief — Video Performance Guide

## Purpose
All staff videos should feel caught in real life, not read from a teleprompter. The writing can be polished; the performance should feel discovered in the moment.

## House cadence
- Speak in thought units, not sentence units.
- Explanatory connective tissue may move quickly. Slow down only for a discovery, reversal, image, or payoff.
- Do not give every sentence equal weight.
- Underplay jokes. A punchline should often be quieter and drier than the setup.
- Use natural thought breaks and breaths rather than theatrical pauses.
- Avoid local-news cadence, generic creator enthusiasm, and “presentation voice.”
- Let the speaker occasionally sound as if they are thinking while talking.
- Preserve slight asymmetry: tiny speed changes, imperfect but intentional emphasis, and brief resets.
- A final line should resolve the thought, not announce that it is the final line.

## Physical performance
- Never lock the eyes on camera for an entire clip.
- Natural blinking, breathing, small posture shifts, and occasional glances toward the environment are desirable.
- Gestures should be infrequent and motivated by the thought.
- During pauses, allow stillness. Do not fill every beat with motion.
- Avoid repeated symmetrical hand gestures and continuous nodding.
- The person should look embedded in the location rather than composited in front of it.

## Environment
- Real, inhabited locations over studios.
- Background action is good when it reinforces the desk/personality.
- Preserve object continuity: no multiplying props, no morphing handheld objects.
- Keep hands empty unless the prop is essential.
- Prefer background movement with simple trajectories: pedestrians, distant foot traffic, ambient room activity.
- Traffic is acceptable only when directionality is visually coherent and distant enough to avoid model artifacts.
- Do not reduce a location to a frozen backdrop just to make generation easier; instead simplify the kinds of movement that are most likely to hallucinate.

## Maude Gannon
Tone: fast, smart, explanatory, slightly caffeinated.

- Start from a real football occurrence, statistic, tendency, or piece of film.
- Move briskly through setup.
- Clip key football phrases into short beats.
- Let tactical reversals change the rhythm.
- Dry disbelief is useful when a number or tendency is absurd.
- Sound like she is talking to someone who already knows football.
- Pronounce football terms naturally: OFF-ense, DEE-fense.

Cadence template:
1. Real occurrence.
2. Why the obvious number is interesting.
3. Short football beat.
4. Mechanism.
5. Reversal.
6. Hidden consequence.
7. Thesis.

## Hollis Crane
Tone: gonzo, observational, literary, slightly exhausted, very alive.

- Let long sentences roll when he is accumulating detail.
- Put color on the strange human details, not the conclusion.
- The delivery may feel like he has been in the scene too long.
- Punchlines are usually underplayed.
- Final lines should often lose energy rather than gain it.
- Never sound like a comedy host waiting for laughter.

Cadence template:
1. Place us somewhere specific.
2. Accumulate weird particulars.
3. Widen into a human observation.
4. Return to the room.
5. Quiet dagger.

## Sabine March
Tone: cool, precise, faintly amused, emotionally economical.

- Controlled does not mean monotone.
- Give sharp observational lines their own shelf.
- Lists should have rhythm and confidence.
- Her amusement should be detectable but never performed.
- Slow slightly at the final reversal or thesis.
- She should sound as if she has seen larger things than the subject currently demanding attention.

Cadence template:
1. State the subject's self-image.
2. Dry corrective observation.
3. Rhythmic evidence.
4. Reversal.
5. Elegant final line.

# Generation-ready beat scripts

## Maude — Flores 78.3
[quick, matter-of-fact]
Brian Flores blitzed on seventy-eight point three percent of Minnesota's defensive snaps in Week One.

[brief beat; interested, not amazed]
That number is absurd on its own — but the more interesting thing is what it forces the OFF-ense to do before the snap.

[clip these]
Five linemen.
Six threats.

[move faster]
So the OFF-ense starts paying for protection. Keep the back in. Maybe the tight end too. Slide this way. Change the route concept.

[small tonal turn]
Now Flores only sends four.

[beat; dry disbelief]
Technically?
The blitz didn't even happen.

[clean explanation]
But Minnesota already removed two eligible receivers from the play.

[slow only slightly]
That's why the seventy-eight point three percent matters. It's not just aggression.

[land the idea, not the performance]
Flores is making offenses defend a blitz before he decides whether to send one.

## Hollis — 1:17 A.M.
[drop us into the room immediately]
It is one seventeen in the morning, and I am sitting in a hotel bar with three men who are angry at people they have never met.

[let this tumble]
One of them is furious at a running back in Indianapolis. Another has refreshed an injury report fourteen times. The third keeps saying the phrase “game script” with the dead eyes of a man explaining a divorce.

[slower; widen]
This is what fantasy football does. It creates a private emotional economy where strangers in helmets can ruin the atmosphere in your home.

[plain, almost prosecutorial]
Nobody here has lost money of consequence. Nobody has lost a job. Nobody has even lost the week yet.

[beat]
And still, grief has entered the room.

[settle]
The bartender has seen worse.

[longer beat; almost throw this away]
He works Sundays.

## Sabine — Rio
[controlled, mildly amused]
The NFL has arrived in Rio with the understandable assumption that it is bringing something enormous.

[short shelf]
Rio is being polite about this.

[rhythmic list; confident]
This is a city that has hosted World Cup finals, Olympic ceremonies, Carnival, Flamengo, Madonna — and several governments' worth of public drama.

[dry]
A football game with excellent branding is not exactly an unprecedented civic event.

[turn]
What's interesting is the reversal. In most American cities, the NFL becomes the center of gravity for the weekend.

[beat]
Here, it has entered somebody else's orbit.

[slow the last two thoughts]
The league came to make Rio part of the NFL calendar.

[clean, understated finish]
For one weekend, the NFL is simply part of Rio's.

## Room-wide lessons

1. **A piece must develop a premise.** Establish the proposed contradiction accurately,
   then let sections expose different consequences of the same larger absurdity. Headers,
   introductions and transitions do real narrative work. A byline cannot rescue a series
   of unrelated mini-recaps.
2. **Research is material, not the voice.** Use the necessary fact as the setup for an
   intelligible, specific and earned consequence. Do not display every fact researched.
   A single play rarely explains an institutional disaster; a statistic repeated in
   another paragraph does not create a second insight. Genuine matchup coverage still
   uses consequential plays, but for its argument rather than a transcription.
3. **Satire is allowed to imagine.** Obviously comic counterfactual motives can be the
   engine of a piece. Do not reduce every joke to a literal assertion demanding evidence.
   Separately verify the factual anchor: who, when, what was said, what a clip shows,
   statistics, history and relationships. Invented motives must not masquerade as sourced
   reporting, quotations, diagnoses, access or established causation.
4. **Give the image or clip a reason to be here.** Commentary should advance an
   interpretation or amplify the contradiction, not preview visible action. Watch the
   correct clip before making claims about it; never substitute a vaguely similar
   recording for the reference Cody supplied.
5. **Develop, do not stack.** Readable paragraphs, comic escalation and connections beat
   repeated cheap one-liners, decorative labels and the same joke twice. A factual setup
   needs a clear payoff; an opaque clever-sounding sentence is not a payoff.
6. **Keep the writer present.** Test whether the perception and argument would remain
   the same after a byline swap. Develop each writer's particular attention, not six
   variants of Hollis. Ruthlessness is useful for this assignment, not compulsory tone
   for every subject or correspondent.
7. **Preserve success during revision.** Mark the actual passages Cody praised, keep
   their function and wording unless a later instruction or verified factual problem
   requires change, and build the weak sections up to them. A successful ending must
   still be earned by the revised whole.

# The Brief — Matchup Recap Editorial Contract

Status: Editorial specification for review; no generator or publication automation activated.

## Principle
The score is evidence, not the story. Each matchup recap is a distinctive, lore-rich miniature human-interest column; the box score already lives elsewhere. Aim for 300–500 words when the story sustains it, without word-count padding.

## Story assignment
Assign by the most interesting narrative, not a default writer.
- Hollis: conflicting receipts, escalation, grievances, reported absurdity
- Sabine: social status, rituals, feuds, embarrassments
- Conrad: ego, power, hypocrisy, failed institutions
- Marnie: strange cultural behavior, names, group-chat absurdity
- Dashiell: incentives, organizational failures, roster economics
- Maude: genuinely important football mechanisms and process

No rule that every writer gets exactly one recap; no default that all go to Maude. Distinct voices must remain distinct.

## Evidence packet (strictly scoped to one edition)
- Provider-verified final result with source time, plus last four weeks of results
- Standings and record trajectories; lineup and bench decisions only when verified
- Relevant confirmed trades, waivers, past columns, public running jokes, league lore
- Manager identities and relationships only with edition-local evidence
- Quotes, chat messages and real-world incidents only when actually supplied or verified

Do not fabricate private messages, conversations, thoughts, emotions, actions, injuries or physical scenes. Metaphor and ironic speculation are fine if grounded in a real event. Never leak a different league's teams or lore.

## Structure
Open on the interesting person or contradiction, not the final score. Build one dramatic through-line with prior receipts and consequences, rather than a list of player totals. Use exact scores sparingly as evidence. Write paragraphs with natural transitions, and close with a memorable development or callback—not a pile of one-line punchlines or a generic conclusion.

## Editorial workflow
Pregame preview and postgame recap are separate assets, each independently approved. Before final scoring, keep the approved preview and display current verified scores. When final, keep the preview labeled pregame, show a pending postgame column until it is approved, and never fabricate a statistical fallback. For approved postgame assets, persist edition, season, week, exact pairing IDs, writer, title, body, source timestamps and approval reference.

## Review rubric
Evaluate narrative originality, actual local lore, source accuracy, writer distinctness, development, humor, and the ending. Reject stories interchangeable across league editions or merely reading the scoreboard. Workshop three representative recaps (one per league) using verified final-week evidence before introducing any new automated publishing.

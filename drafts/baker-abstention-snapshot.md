---
title: Baker Mayfield and the Comparative Merits of Abstention
subtitle: An afternoon that satisfied the league’s scheduling requirements.
byline: Maude Gannon
status: staging-only-preservation
---

Baker Mayfield played an entire NFL game on Sunday and finished with zero passing yards.

Not approximately zero. Not a small number being rounded down for emphasis. Zero. He started at zero, remained available throughout, and brought the original zero safely home.

Tampa Bay won 16–0.

That should be enough. A starting quarterback has completed no passes, gained no passing yards, played the whole game and won. There is no need to make this more unreasonable.

This was not the worst-rated passing performance in the game.

Michael Penix Jr. completed eight passes for 125 yards. He also threw four interceptions. His passer rating was 12.0.

Baker’s was 39.6.

The man who had not passed successfully was more than twenty-seven points clear in the evaluation of passing. I am not demanding an investigation into the formula. I would, however, like someone to check whether the word *passer* is still doing any work.

Change three of Penix’s interceptions to incompletions, preserving every completion and every yard, and his rating rises to 34.2. Still below Baker. Give Baker twenty-one additional attempts and make every one incomplete: still 39.6. We have improved one hypothetical performance, prolonged the other, and failed to restore order.

The actual football was having similar difficulties.

Mayfield fumbled three times and recovered all three. Three official pass attempts; three occasions on which he had to retrieve the football. He was keeping himself involved.

[Scott Smith’s account for the Buccaneers](https://www.buccaneers.com/news/falcons-bucs-week-8-recap-postgame-report-2026) described rain alternating between steady and torrential. Two of Baker’s three official passes were dropped. By halftime, Penix had completed two passes and thrown three interceptions, then fumbled Atlanta’s final snap of the half and recovered it himself.

Thirty minutes of football. The two quarterbacks had combined for two completions. The man responsible for both had also thrown all three interceptions.

There is weather that disrupts a football game. This was a football game struggling to establish that the weather was its largest problem.

Consider Atlanta’s defense.

Bucky Irving rushed for 170 yards. Kenny Gainwell, who entered Sunday with 149 across seven games, added 140. Between them, they gained 310 yards against a defense that had very little else to concern itself with. A reasonably determined member of the grounds crew could have picked up forty on the way to checking a drain.

And this was not happening while Baker terrorized the secondary. Tampa Bay completed no passes. The rain (and abhorrent football) had practically removed an entire category of offense from consideration.

Apparently it was going to have to tackle the famously uncontainable Kenny Gainwell as well.

On Tampa Bay’s touchdown drive, the first five runs were all recorded around left end. Eighty-six yards. The Bucs changed running backs twice before changing the listed direction.

The football had been making its whereabouts increasingly available.

On the sixth play, Irving went over right guard for the touchdown.

A surprise ending.

Tampa Bay finished with 324 rushing yards and 322 yards of total offense. Baker had taken a two-yard sack. The passing game, having produced nothing, had even gone so far as to levy a small tax on the people producing everything.

Atlanta continued with the full version of football. Twenty-four passes. Eight completions. Four interceptions. No points.

Sixty minutes had been allotted to establish which of these teams was better. It is unfortunate that there was no provision for establishing which one was less bad and letting everybody leave early.

Eventually the clock reached zero, joining several other important measurements.

At last, a zero that did something for the audience.
